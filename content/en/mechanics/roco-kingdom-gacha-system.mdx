### What "Zero Paywalls" Probably Means for You

"Zero paywalls" does not mean "zero monetization." In practice, free-to-play creature collectors usually monetize three things: convenience, cosmetics, and randomized rewards. The first two are harmless. The third is where spending discipline matters. Treat the phrase as a promise about *content access* — that you will not hit a locked door requiring payment to continue the story — and not as a promise that every Jini skin will be free.

## How Gacha Mechanics Usually Work in a Creature Collector

If you have played any free-to-play collector, the vocabulary will feel familiar. The table below defines the terms you will see in every launch-day discussion thread, so you can read patch notes and community guides without guessing.

| Term | What It Means | Why It Matters Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Banner** | A time-limited pool of featured rewards | Determines whether your favorite Jini appearance is available *now* |
| **Pull / roll** | One paid or free attempt at the pool | The core unit of spending; usually sold in discounted bundles |
| **Rate** | The stated probability of each rarity tier | Always published, sometimes buried — find it before spending |
| **Pity** | A guaranteed reward after a set number of pulls | Your safety net; never spend without knowing where your counter sits |
| **Soft pity** | Rising odds as you approach the guarantee | Often where smart spenders concentrate their pulls |
| **Rate-up** | A featured item with boosted odds | The difference between a 50% and a 0.7% chance is enormous |
| **Dupe** | A duplicate of something you already own | Determines whether duplicates convert into useful currency |
| **Spark / exchange** | A shop where pity tokens buy the item directly | The most reliable path for unlucky players |
| **Split currency** | Free and paid currencies that cannot be mixed | Free currency usually cannot buy the premium banner |

The practical takeaway: in any game with these systems, **the pity counter is more valuable than luck**. Players who track it consistently get the item they want for far less than players who pull impulsively on every new banner.

### A Practical Pulling Plan You Can Use on Day One

- **Set a hard monthly budget before launch.** Write it down. A number you decided calmly beats a number you decide at 2 a.m.
- **Find the published rates first.** Screenshot them. If rates are not visible, do not spend.
- **Bank free currency for the first two weeks.** Launch banners are rarely the best value; you will not know what you want until you have played the Academy and PvP.
- **Only pull on banners where you want the featured item**, never for "value."
- **Stop at the guarantee, not before it** — partial progress toward a pity you never finish is the most common waste in gacha games.
- **Track your pity counter in a notes app.** Memory is unreliable across multi-week banners.
- **Never convert premium currency into cosmetics you can earn.** Check the free track first.

## Community Reports and Red Flags to Watch Before Launch

Player experience from the genre — and early discussion around Roco Kingdom's test recruitment — points to a handful of warning signs worth monitoring once the game goes live. None of these are confirmed problems; they are the things that separate a healthy system from an exploitative one.

- **Hidden or vague rates.** Regulators in several markets require disclosure. If rates are not published, treat that as a red flag.
- **No visible pity counter.** A guarantee you cannot track is not a guarantee.
- **Free and paid currency that look identical** but cannot be combined — a classic source of accidental spending.
- **FOMO timers on every banner.** Constant limited windows push impulsive purchases.
- **Power tied to duplicates.** If a duplicate Jini meaningfully boosts battle strength, the cosmetic-first promise is broken.

One reassuring signal: the game uses Easy Anti-Cheat and requires an account-bound client, which at minimum means your purchases are tied to a secured profile rather than a throwaway guest login. Keep two-factor authentication on whatever account system Morefun Studios uses at launch.
