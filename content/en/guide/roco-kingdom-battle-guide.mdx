## How Turn-Based Combat Works in Roco Kingdom

Roco Kingdom runs on classic turn-based rules, and the simplicity is the point. Each Jinie carries four skills into battle, both sides act in sequence, and every action draws from the same stamina pool. There is no hidden real-time layer working against you — the depth comes from what you do with four buttons and one limited resource.

| Mechanic | How it works | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Turn order | Both sides act in sequence, one action each turn | Lets you plan a full exchange instead of reacting |
| Skill slots | Four skills per Jinie, chosen before the fight | Forces real trade-offs in moveset design |
| Stamina bar | Attacks consume stamina, shown as stars | The core resource that decides tempo |
| Resting | Low stamina forces a recovery turn | Punishes over-attacking and rewards prediction |
| Matchup hints | The UI flags whether a move hits a weakness | Removes type-chart memorization from the skill gap |
| Level normalization | PvP standardizes battle levels | Shifts outcomes toward strategy, not stats |

The matchup hint system deserves a note. Because the game tells you before you commit whether a move lands on a weakness, you never lose a fight to a forgotten type chart. But your opponent sees the same hints, which means experienced players deliberately bait attacks into resistances. Three habits separate beginners from consistent winners:

- Check the hint, then ask what your opponent expects you to click.
- Never open with your most expensive skill just because it is super effective.
- Track your stamina as carefully as your opponent's health bar.

## Stamina Management Is the Real Skill Ceiling

Every attack costs stamina, and when the bar runs low your Jinie has to burn a turn resting. That single rule turns each battle into a negotiation over resources. A player who attacks every turn will always run dry first against someone who plans two turns ahead.

| Your situation | Best default | The risk |
|---|---|---|
| Full bar, clear weakness | Cheap, efficient attack | Overkill wastes stamina you will want later |
| Half bar, healthy opponent | Mid-cost skill, hold one attack in reserve | Running dry hands them a free turn |
| Nearly empty, opponent likely to switch | Rest now and bank the tempo | They stay in and punish the free turn |
| Empty against a faster threat | Switch to a fresh Jinie | You give up board position |
| Opponent rested last turn | Expect a big hit — switch or absorb it | Eating a full-power attack at low stamina |

The signature play in any Roco Kingdom battle guide worth reading is the predictive rest. If you expect your opponent to switch — because their active Jinie is losing the matchup — you can rest on that same turn and enter the next exchange with a full bar while they spent their turn switching. You traded nothing for a full resource refill.

Just as important: do not dump the bar. Keeping one attack's worth of stamina in reserve means you always have a response if the fight goes sideways, and it keeps the threat of a real hit on the table.

## Reading Matchup Hints and Building Bloodline Coverage

Visible hints tell you about the move you are hovering over. They do not tell you about the fight two turns ahead, and that gap is where teams fall apart. Coverage is the answer, and Bloodline is the tool.

Bloodline skills can overwrite a Jinie's normal skill set, letting it learn moves from another element class. In practice, that turns a creature that would normally get walled by one specific element into one that carries an answer to it. With 18 elements in play, this is how you patch holes without rebuilding your whole roster.

| Coverage problem | Bloodline approach | Trade-off to accept |
|---|---|---|
| One element walls your main attacker | Teach an off-element move for that specific matchup | You give up a slot that covered something else |
| Your team has no answer to a popular threat | Convert one mid-role Jinie into a dedicated check | That Jinie becomes narrower overall |
| You keep losing to a single switch-in | Add coverage that hits the switch-in, not the active | Less raw damage in neutral exchanges |
| Your team is all one class | Spread Bloodlines across two classes | Slightly lower synergy, much better flexibility |

A simple audit before you queue: list the four elements you see most often, then check whether every member of your team has at least one move that threatens each of them. If not, that is your Bloodline project.

## Team Building for PvP: Elements, Classes, and Roles

PvP in Roco Kingdom revolves around class and elemental matchups, and levels are standardized so stat gaps do not decide fights. That means your roster needs roles, not just power.

| Role | Job in the fight | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Opener | Win the first exchange and set tempo | Reliable cheap attacks, good neutral matchups |
| Wall breaker | Punch through defensive switch-ins | High-damage coverage, ideally off-element |
| Pivot | Absorb a bad matchup and reset | Resistances that let it come in safely |
| Closer | Finish a weakened team | Speed or priority-style pressure at low stamina |

A balanced core usually runs one opener, one pivot, one breaker, and one closer, with the last slots flexing toward whatever you keep losing to. Player experience suggests the most common team-building mistake is stacking four attackers with overlapping coverage — it feels powerful until one wall shuts down the entire lineup.

## A Practical Turn-by-Turn Battle Plan

If you want a repeatable process rather than vague advice, use this sequence every battle. It works for story fights and carries directly into PvP.

| Step | Action | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check the matchup hint on your safest cheap attack | Establish whether you actually win the exchange |
| 2 | Estimate your opponent's stamina from their last two turns | Predict when they must rest |
| 3 | If they are near empty, attack; if they may switch, rest | Convert reads into resource advantage |
| 4 | Spend your reserve only when it closes out a Jinie | Avoid wasteful burst |
| 5 | Switch early on a losing matchup, not after a hit | Preserve health for the next exchange |
| 6 | Save your Bloodline coverage answer for their wall | Keep the threat alive until it matters |

Run that loop for a few sessions and the decisions become automatic. Most improvement in Roco Kingdom battle guide terms comes from step two — simply counting your opponent's stamina turns guessing into planning.

## FAQ

**Is Roco Kingdom PvP pay-to-win?**
No, based on the publisher's stated design. Levels are normalized in PvP, there is no gacha system, and Jinies can be obtained through normal play. A good Roco Kingdom battle guide should always start from that assumption: outcomes come down to matchups, team composition, and reads.

**How does the stamina bar work?**
Every attack costs stamina, displayed as stars. When the bar runs low, your Jinie must spend a turn resting to recover. The advanced play is resting on a turn you expect your opponent to switch, so you refill while they waste an action.

**Do I need to memorize the type chart?**
No. The battle UI shows matchup hints before you commit to an attack, flagging whether a move hits a weakness. You still need to think about what your opponent expects, but raw memorization is not required.

**How many skills does each Jinie have?**
Four. Bloodline skills can overwrite those normal skills, letting a Jinie learn moves from another element class — which is the main way players cover bad matchups without swapping team members.
